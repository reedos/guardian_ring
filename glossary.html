Orbits and geometry

Geostationary orbit

A circular orbit over the equator that moves with Earth’s rotation and keeps a satellite above the same ground location.

  • Geostationary Earth Orbit Satellite (GEO)Definition paragraph

Geosynchronous orbit

An orbit with a period equal to Earth’s rotation relative to the stars. The circular, equatorial, prograde case is geostationary.

  • Catalog of Earth Satellite OrbitsHigh Earth Orbit

HEO

Highly elliptical orbit: an elongated path with substantially different closest and farthest distances from Earth. This site’s HEO example is a generic teaching ellipse.

  • Catalog of Earth Satellite OrbitsHighly Elliptical Orbits

LEO and MEO

Low Earth orbit and medium Earth orbit are orbit families. Their selected altitudes here are teaching inputs, not the parameters of a military program.

  • Catalog of Earth Satellite OrbitsLow Earth Orbit; Medium Earth Orbit

Semi-major axis

Half the longest diameter of an ellipse. In the two-body model it sets the orbital period; it is measured from the ellipse’s center, not from Earth’s surface.

  • How Orbital Motion is CalculatedOpening orbital-elements discussion

Eccentricity

A measure of an orbit’s shape. A circular orbit has zero eccentricity; increasing elliptic eccentricity makes the ellipse more elongated.

  • How Orbital Motion is CalculatedOpening orbital-elements discussion

Apogee and perigee

The farthest and closest points, respectively, in an orbit around Earth. The HEO calculation displayed here uses apogee.

  • How Orbital Motion is CalculatedPolar equation and motion near perigee and apogee

Inclination

The angle between an orbital plane and its reference plane. Earth’s equatorial plane is the usual reference for Earth satellites.

  • How Orbital Motion is CalculatedThe Orbit in Space

Nadir

The direction toward the surface point directly below the observer. The model uses that point to define its reference distance.

Sidereal day

Earth’s rotation period measured relative to the distant celestial reference frame. It supplies the general geosynchronous reference period.

  • Astrodynamic ParametersParameter table: mean sidereal day

Light and thermal radiation

Infrared

Electromagnetic radiation at wavelengths longer than visible light. Infrared instruments can measure thermal emission from warm objects.

  • Electromagnetic MathElectromagnetic spectrum and The Energy of Light activities

Photon

A quantum of electromagnetic radiation. A photon’s energy depends on its frequency, and therefore on its wavelength.

  • Electromagnetic MathActivity 47, PDF p. 112

Wavelength

The spatial repeat distance of a wave. In vacuum, wavelength multiplied by frequency equals the speed of light.

  • Electromagnetic MathActivity 47, PDF p. 112, problem 4

Spectral band

A selected interval of wavelengths. An instrument channel has a particular spectral response; the model’s teaching intervals are ideal rectangles, not instrument responses.

  • Thermal Infrared Sensor (TIRS)Spectral Bands

SWIR, MWIR and LWIR

Short-wave, mid-wave and long-wave infrared. The model uses these broad labels to organize illustrative wavelengths, without adopting universal boundaries or a real sensor bandpass.

  • Infrared Detectors Overview in the Short Wave Infrared to Far Infrared for CLARREO MissionSections A.1–A.3 and Table 1

How this project uses the term →

Molecular emission band

A region of the spectrum associated with radiation from a molecule. The cited civil combustion study identifies bands of carbon dioxide and water vapor.

  • Determination of primary-zone smoke concentrations from spectral radiance measurements in gas turbine combustorsPrinted p. 5 / PDF p. 7, gaseous combustion products paragraph

Atmospheric absorption and windows

Atmospheric gases absorb some wavelengths more strongly than others. A window is a region with greater transmission; this site does not calculate a transmission curve.

  • Remote SensingAbsorption Bands and Atmospheric Windows

Blackbody

An ideal thermal radiator whose spectrum depends on its temperature. The model’s uniform blackbody is a teaching source, not a measured Earth scene or plume.

  • Planck FunctionFirst formula image

How this project uses the term →

Spectral radiance

Radiant power per projected area, per solid angle, and per wavelength interval. Stating the wavelength unit matters: a per-meter density differs from a per-micrometer density.

  • Planck FunctionWavelength calculator and first formula image

Band radiance

Spectral radiance integrated across a stated wavelength interval. The result retains area and solid-angle units; it is not power collected by a detector.

  • Planck FunctionWavelength-form Planck law

How this project uses the term →

Photon radiance

Radiance expressed as a photon rate per projected area and solid angle. Each wavelength’s energy radiance is divided by the energy of one photon before integration.

  • Electromagnetic MathActivity 47, PDF p. 112

How this project uses the term →

Diffraction

The spreading and interference associated with a wave passing through an aperture. The ideal circular-aperture example gives an angular scale, not the measured resolution of a real instrument.

  • Electromagnetic MathActivity 46, PDF p. 110

How this project uses the term →

Vacuum light time

The time light takes to cross a stated distance in vacuum. Processing and communications routing add other delays, so this is not a warning-system latency.

Instruments and architecture

OPIR

Overhead persistent infrared: the term used in the cited public architecture documents for space-based infrared observation supporting warning and related missions.

  • Missile Warning Satellites: Comprehensive Cost and Schedule Information Would Enhance Congressional OversightBackground, printed pp. 3–4

SBIRS

Space Based Infrared System. The cited public GAO report describes spacecraft in geosynchronous and highly elliptical orbits and an associated ground segment.

  • Missile Warning Satellites: Comprehensive Cost and Schedule Information Would Enhance Congressional OversightPrinted p. 4 / PDF p. 8

Bus and payload

The bus is the spacecraft platform supporting the mission equipment. An infrared payload, a mission processor, and communications equipment have distinct roles in the public architecture described by GAO.

  • Missile Warning Satellites: Space Development Agency Should Be More Realistic and Transparent About Risks to Capability DeliveryPWSA-Enabling Technologies and Processes, opening description

Ground segment

The receiving, spacecraft-operations, and mission-data-processing parts of a space system on the ground. A drawn ground marker represents those roles, not an actual facility layout.

  • Missile Warning Satellites: Comprehensive Cost and Schedule Information Would Enhance Congressional OversightPrinted p. 1 / PDF p. 5 and printed p. 8 / PDF p. 12

FORGE

Future Operationally Resilient Ground Evolution. GAO’s historical program description assigns the planned system spacecraft-operations and mission-data-processing roles.

  • Missile Warning Satellites: Comprehensive Cost and Schedule Information Would Enhance Congressional OversightPrinted p. 1 / PDF p. 5, opening paragraph

ABI

Advanced Baseline Imager, the civil imaging instrument of the GOES-R satellite series. Its published channel and observing-cadence figures describe that instrument alone.

  • Advanced Baseline Imager (ABI)Instrument description

TIRS-2

Thermal Infrared Sensor 2, the Landsat 9 civil thermal instrument. Its published detector, optics, and cooling specifications remain attached to that named instrument.

  • TIRS-2 Testing: A Landsat 9 Instrument Takes ShapeInstrument design and cryocooler paragraphs

Focal plane

The region where an optical system forms its image and detector assemblies receive the light. The civil examples show that instrument-specific optical and detector designs vary.

  • GOES-R Series Data Book, Revision APrinted pp. 3-8 and 3-11 / PDF pp. 36 and 39

QWIP

Quantum well infrared photodetector, a detector technology used in the named TIRS civil instruments. Selecting this material family does not assign an operating temperature.

  • Thermal Infrared Sensor (TIRS)Design section

HgCdTe and InSb

Mercury cadmium telluride and indium antimonide: infrared detector materials discussed in the opened NASA detector review. A material name alone is not a temperature or performance specification.

  • Infrared Detectors Overview in the Short Wave Infrared to Far Infrared for CLARREO MissionPDF pp. 3–7, sections A.1–A.3

Cryocooler

A refrigerator that removes heat from a cold region. The cold-stage temperature, heat lift, and required input power depend on the particular device and operating conditions.

  • Review of Refrigeration Methods (submitted chapter)Opening refrigeration discussion, PDF pp. 2–3

Carnot coefficient of performance

The ideal refrigeration limit relating heat removed at the cold temperature to required work. A real cooler’s efficiency and heat load are separate quantities.

  • Review of Refrigeration Methods (submitted chapter)PDF pp. 2–3, equation (1) and definitions
