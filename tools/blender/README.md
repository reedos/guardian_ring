# Hardware asset pipeline

The Explorer loads ten versioned GLBs from `public/models/`. Their `build-*.py` scripts own the hardware geometry; JavaScript supplies lighting, role labels, teaching paths, camera interaction, and source-linked component anatomy. Civil references establish component identities and selected counts, not the drawings' dimensions or complete construction.

## Builders and assemblies

`hardware-detail.py` supplies shared structural decks, enclosures, circuit boards, connectors, mechanisms, thermal links, and solar-array geometry. `build-satellite.py` uses these to expose twelve parent assemblies with named internal equipment.

`build-payload.py`, `payload-expansion.py`, and `payload-electronics.py` produce payload v17 as an integrated teaching instrument. Its sensor unit electronics (SUE), common instrument electronics unit (EU), and cooler control electronics (CCE) have coherent enclosures, mounted boards, bulkhead connectors, harnesses, and conductive mounting paths. Thirteen stable learning selections identify components and functions within this instrument; they do not represent thirteen separate physical assemblies. Scan drives, encoder/bearing supports, focus and port-cover hardware, calibration examples, stationary beamsplitters/filters/stops, the cold detector package (with a cold fold mirror over its top cold-stop aperture), and the conduction-cooled cryocooler path remain part of the shared design. Optics, filters, mirrors and the displaced cold module stand on posts or legs that reach the bench. The port cover is spring-deployed after release; the solar-calibration cover is a separate motor-driven mechanism. No filter wheel or physical deep-space calibration target is implied.

The Explorer starts with the electronics enclosures assembled. Its Inside view hides only the exact authored cover roles `SensorElectronicsCover`, `InstrumentElectronicsCover`, and `CoolerElectronicsCover`, including their lid trim and fasteners. Matching uses the exact `assetRole` or exact cover-parent name, never a prefix that could remove neighboring hardware. Boards, chassis walls, mounting feet, connectors, harnesses, and thermal interfaces stay in place. Selecting an internal function or starting its lesson opens this view; the same thirteen learning selections remain available.

`build-focal-plane.py` produces focal-plane v9 with eight parent anchors: array, readout, cold stage, shield, flex, carrier, bias/timing, and thermal feedback. It distinguishes the cold detector/ROIC package from warm video electronics and shows a separate thermometer-to-controller-to-cooler path. Board packages, repeated elements, traces, harnesses, and thermal supports are representative; no pixel format or ADC implementation is inferred.

The remaining builders produce the ring, detector element, plume, ground segment, ABI, TIRS-2, and atmospheric illustration. `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` lists the complete asset inventory. No manufacturer logos, invented part numbers, or assigned hardware masses are permitted.

## Export and story stills

Run builders with Blender 5.2 in background mode. GLBs export Y-up with named anchors and drawing metadata. Every rebuild requires a matching version increase in the builder/export metadata and the scene's `?v=N` URL; current assets are `satellite.glb?v=14`, `payload.glb?v=18`, `focal-plane.glb?v=10`, `plume.glb?v=7`, `abi.glb?v=11`, `tirs2.glb?v=10`, `atmosphere.glb?v=6`, `earth-orbits.glb?v=7` and `ground.glb?v=6`. The builders export plain GLBs; run `npm run models:compress` after exporting to meshopt-compress them (positions, node transforms, names, extras, materials and textures stay exact; normals and UVs are quantized, 12 bits for normals). The viewer and the tests decode with `src/scenes/gltf-loader.js`. Meshopt compression took the ten GLBs from 12.06 MB to 6.25 MB (about 4.4 MB to 2.4 MB gzipped), which moved every key one step (satellite 13 to 14, and so on) without changing a builder's geometry version. Satellite v11 places the separated radiator on the +X array-axis face, points the star tracker away from the Earth-facing aperture, and draws the TT&C reflector as a concave dish; v12 closes the -X face with a blanketed closeout and cuts away only the viewer-facing +Z closeout, marking its cut edges. Focal-plane v9 has an opaque cutaway cold shield with a cold-stop aperture plate and window, and a visible braided strap from the cold finger. ABI v9 and TIRS-2 v8 sit inside sectioned instrument housings. Cut edges use one section color in every model, a drawing convention.

Blender round 1 (10/14/2026): the shared chassis finish is lifted one step (`dark` and `black` in `hardware-detail.py`) so enclosures keep a value step under real-time lighting; the Explorer's hardware lighting profile was raised to match. Satellite v13 draws the sunshade as a flared, blanketed hood with a black baffled interior, seats the telescope on rails and saddle clamps, hangs the TT&C dish on a hinged boom and thickens the array yokes; it stays a generic representative spacecraft, not a GOES-R model. ABI v10 uses a stepped, hooded housing with a flared optical-port sunshield, and TIRS-2 v9 an arched, blanketed housing, so the two civil twins have different silhouettes (outline and finish are drawing choices; neither is a published figure). Payload v17 puts routed lines on standoff clips and gives the slender mirror and filter posts footed bases and braces. Plume v6 adds a luminous core with a drawn shock-diamond pattern and an expanding neck near the source, is authored in its displayed orientation (the runtime no longer flips it) and carries a dark inset plate behind the enlarged molecule diagrams. Atmosphere v5 places the two enlarged symbols inside the air volume, smaller and clear of the slab edge. Earth-orbits v6 gives the spacecraft proxy the satellite palette and a round telescope barrel (bounds, triangle budget and ports unchanged) and makes the atmospheric-limb shell translucent so the textured Earth is visible in any viewer; the Earth day and night maps were already embedded in the GLB, and `build-orbits.py` reuses those derivatives when the gitignored NASA originals are absent. Cryocooler housings are smooth and conduction-mounted; no air fins are drawn. A successful export does not establish browser acceptance.

`render-authored.py -- satellite payload focal-plane pixel plume` imports the shipped GLBs and renders separate desktop and phone story views. It reuses the approved lighting/fitting helpers without executing the old prototype geometry builders. For payload, it hides only the same exact cover roles used by the Explorer's Inside view, leaving mounted internals and connections intact. All twelve story stills were re-rendered with `tools/render-story-stills.mjs` from satellite v13, payload v17, focal-plane v9 and plume v6 on 10/14/2026 and use the `?v=blr4` image key. GLB versions and image cache keys are separate sequences. Rerun the relevant stills after a GLB changes so the story and interactive cutaway agree.

`build-orbit-look.py` supplies the ring story render. Source PNGs stay under ignored `research/look/renders/`; `encode-look.py` uses Python/Pillow to create the twelve WebPs in `public/look/`. The historical NASA Earth textures remain under `research/look/textures/`; the interactive globe embeds its delivery derivatives. Credits, hashes, and drawing assumptions are recorded in `research/look-assets.md`.

The responsive system diagrams are HTML/SVG authored in `src/pages/system-diagrams.js`, not Blender renders. They reuse the component catalog's evidence and illustrate selected functional connections. The payload v13 revision completed all twenty full-scope built-preview browser gate commands on the real RTX 5090 on 10/02/2026. `research/gate-results.md` records accepted build SHA-256 `fd3463e001d9a178003bab65f1f226b87c0732f8160756f400fcb708cf77e01d`, 156 passing unit tests, a clean typecheck, and zero evidence-audit problems. These are local results, not remote CI or deployment status. Model or camera changes still require fresh GPU gates and visual inspection.
